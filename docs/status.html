Open questions

What does the draft room's --selecting modifier mean while bidding is running? not blocking

Why it matters. The seat that put this player up, or the seat due to put up the next one. Those are different teams, so nominated_by is deliberately left unwired rather than guessed — picking wrong would fire the stale-order banner on nearly every pick.

How it gets answered. tools/draft_watch.py already flashes it on change. One nomination landing in a rehearsal settles it.

Does the real league's mDraftDetail backfill when the real draft completes? not blocking

Why it matters. It would be a usable end-of-draft reconciliation pass. A practice draft can never answer it — ESPN reaps the ephemeral league the moment the draft ends.

How it gets answered. Only draft day can answer it. Nothing depends on it: the live path is the DOM either way.

safe_legal_bid's optimistic ceiling has never fired live. not blocking

Why it matters. No pick has yet arrived without a price, so `legal max` has never shown its `+`. Unit-tested and dry-run, but not seen in real traffic.

How it gets answered. Will exercise itself the first time ESPN sends a pick with no winning price.

advise() cost note

~40 ms at 100 sales, of which ~34 ms is nomination_plan scanning the remaining board. Fires once per user action, so it is invisible today. A surface that polls it many times a second is the case that would change that.

The plan is calibrated to this league, not to an archetype constant note

The generated bench reserve was $20 — 10% of budget, an archetype default with nothing behind it. Four seasons say the median team here spends $5 on its cheapest five picks, which is also the arithmetic floor: 5 bench slots at the $1 minimum. Rebuilt with --bench 5, and the freed $15 went back into the split at market shares. The plan now sits within a few dollars of the league median at every position: WR $85 vs $91, RB $82 vs $74, TE $13 vs $7, QB $12 vs $9. max_on_one_player set to $75 by hand — the priciest player in four seasons went $80, so it still binds at the very top, which is the point of having one. Both overrides are reproducible flags, not TOML edits: `ffa strategy init --force --bench 5 --max-player 75` rebuilds this plan from scratch.

The dashboard is a second process, on purpose note

It never opens a DraftStore and never takes the pid lock: it replays the journal and serves the same build_view() payload every other surface reads. Two consequences worth keeping. The engine keeps exactly one writer, so nothing the dashboard does can corrupt journal ordering. And a rendering bug at pick 90 costs you a browser tab rather than the process recording your draft. Reading a file under active append is safe here for a specific reason: the journal already tolerates a torn trailing line, because that is what a crash leaves behind, and a concurrent reader hits exactly that case.

The dashboard now follows the design handoff note

Rebuilt in src/ffa/dashboard/page.html from the designer's bundle: status bar, on-the-block with the three metric cards, the threat matrix, plan health, runway. The matrix is the piece worth having — positions across, teams down, cell fill is what that team could pay, and a satisfied position goes dark, so a rich team with no gap reads as harmless without any cross-referencing. Three regions are deliberately inert because they need the inference layer that does not exist: rival bid *estimates* and their rationale, the analysis log (capability 6), and the assistant. They render as designed empty states naming what is missing, per the designer's own instruction to degrade to arithmetic rather than delay the page.

The feed is a fold, not a log note

Capability 6 appends nothing anywhere. It folds the journal and emits where something crossed, which means undo removes an entry rather than contradicting it, and the feed can never disagree with the board beside it because both come from the same events. Six rules, all arithmetic, all bounded: over a full 168-pick synthetic draft it produces about 30 entries. The `room` rule originally reported every decrement and printed eleven near-identical lines in a row; it now fires on three milestones. A test runs every rule over a whole draft and fails if any of them scales with picks.

Capability 9 counts demand the way a roster actually works note

A position is dangerous when the teams who can still act on it outnumber what is left. Two counting decisions carry it. Demand is counted against the position's own starting slot, never the flex: has_starter_gap folds the flex in, which is right for 'could they start this player' and would otherwise add twelve phantom contenders to RB, WR and TE at once and make every position look squeezed from pick one. And a contender must be able to pay — needing one and being able to buy one are different things. The list is empty for most of a draft by construction, which is the point.
